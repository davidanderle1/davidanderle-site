Volatility Cascade Engine

A deterministic Python prototype exploring how leverage, margin thresholds, forced liquidation, overlapping exposures, and endogenous price impact can amplify a shock in a synthetic system.

Boundary. The model is synthetic and uncalibrated. It does not predict market crashes, reproduce a broker’s full margin system, estimate real-world systemic risk, or provide investment advice.

Result in one sentence

The sweep demonstrates a plausible feedback mechanism under selected assumptions: overlapping leveraged portfolios can turn an external shock into a larger internal drawdown once margin-triggered selling begins.

Research question

Can a stylized system of leveraged portfolios with overlapping holdings generate amplification after an external price shock when the only feedback channel is margin-triggered liquidation and price impact?

My exact role

I built and published the Python baseline, defined the synthetic balance sheets and exposure templates, implemented the iterative liquidation and price-impact loop, generated the outputs, and wrote the public case study and report.

Data and provenance

No broker, market, customer, or institutional data are used. The six funds and five assets are fictional. Equity, leverage, thresholds, liquidation fractions, market depth, and portfolio weights are model inputs.

Method

  1. Construct synthetic balance sheets from equity and target leverage.
  2. Shock the price of one asset and mark every portfolio to market.
  3. Classify a fund as distressed when marked leverage reaches its threshold.
  4. Sell a fixed fraction of distressed positions.
  5. Apply aggregate sales through an exponential price-impact function.
  6. Repeat until stable or the twelve-round cap is reached.

Validation and falsification attempts

  • The deterministic rerun on 29 July 2026 reproduced the released scenario table.
  • System equity loss is non-decreasing over the tested shock grid.
  • No fund is distressed in the 2%–14% scenarios; the first classification occurs at 16%.
  • The script’s simple detector reported no “obvious threshold,” so the public record does not claim one.

Limitations

  • All inputs are synthetic and uncalibrated.
  • Debt, margin thresholds, liquidation fractions, and market depth are stylized and fixed.
  • There is no bid/ask spread, transaction cost, order book, options convexity, funding-liquidity process, or default waterfall.
  • The only network channel is shared asset exposure.
  • Results are sensitive to the selected thresholds, impact coefficient, depth, and exposure templates.

What would make the model stronger

Separate exogenous and endogenous loss, implement target deleveraging, add state-dependent liquidity and transaction costs, run sensitivity surfaces, test alternative network structures, and compare the mechanism with permitted empirical or historically reconstructed scenarios.
